Clip Path and Mask Examples

clip-path

circle()

Circle: 35%

Clips/crops the image into a circle shape with a hard border. 100% shows the whole image as it is, 0% makes it such a small circle, it is non-existent.

clip-path

ellipse()

clip-path: ellipse(40% 25% at center);

Clips/crops the image into an ellipse shape with a hard border. ellipse( radius-x% radius-y% at (position) )

clip-path

inset()

clip-path: inset(8% round 1.5rem);

Inset if useful for cropping edges off an image or creating a round rectangle that differs from border-radius (clipped content is truly invisible, not just hidden behind rounded corners).

clip-path

polygon()

clip-path: inset(8% round 1.5rem);

Polygon is the most flexible because you define each vertex as an x/y percentage or length, letting you create triangles, hexagons, arrows, diagonal slices, or any other straight-edged shape.

When to Use Which

The choice between clipping and masking comes down to the kind of edge you need:

  • Your card relies on box-shadow to separate it from the background. Forced Colors Mode removes box shadows, so the card's edge disappears.
  • Soft fades, gradual transparency, or complex shapes from images: Use mask-image. It handles partial transparency that clip paths cannot express.
  • Animatable shape reveals: Use clip-path with matching vertex counts. Mask images are not smoothly animatable in the same way.
  • Broad browser support without prefixes: clip-path wins. mask-image still requires -webkit- in most shipping browsers.

In practice, clip-path handles about 80% of non-rectangular layout needs. Reach for mask-image when you need the soft edges or image-based shapes that clipping cannot produce.

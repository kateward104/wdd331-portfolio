Aspect Ratio and Object Fit

Learn about aspect ratios and positioning images in CSS.

object-fit

object-fit: fill

Fill (default): stretches the content to fill the container exactly, distorting the ratio if the container is a different proportion.

object-fit

object-fit: cover

Cover scales the content up until it covers the entire container, cropping the overflow. The ratio is preserved. This is the most common value for card thumbnails and hero images.

object-fit

object-fit: contain

Contains scales the content down until it fits entirely inside the container without cropping. The ratio is preserved but there may be empty space (letterboxing).

object-position

Using object-position

This controls where the content is anchored within its container when it does not fill it exactly, or when cover crops it.

object-position

object-position custom

You can enter in an x and y percentage to more precisely target the area you want to focus on in your image.

object-position: X% Y%;

This image is set to object-position: 50% 60%; to get more of the flowers in frame.

combination of aspect-ratio and object-position

Ideal Image Thumbnail

width: 100%;
aspect-ratio: 16 / 9;
object-fit: cover;
object-position: center;
display: block;

Summary

  • fill: stretches the content to fill the container exactly
    cover: scales the content up until it covers the entire container, cropping the overflow. The ratio is preserved.
    contain: scales the content down until it fits entirely inside the container without cropping. The ratio is preserved but there may be empty space (letterboxing).
  • Aspect-ratio controls the cropping and format your image/video is seen in. ex: square, rectangle, vertical, horizontal, etc. Use it, instead of the old padding-top: 56.25% rule, to get a better, and more accurate 16 / 9 that is more responsive to mobile.
  • Object-positioning allows you to move the image around in the frame once it is cropped/hidden with aspect-ratio. You can do this with named areas like top center, center right, or bottom left. You can also use X% and Y% for even more precise focus on an area of an image.

Understanding how these all work together will help you to better control your images and create more professional websites.
